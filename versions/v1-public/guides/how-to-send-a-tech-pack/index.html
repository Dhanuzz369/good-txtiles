How to send a tech pack to a garment manufacturer

A tech pack is the instruction file a factory uses to make your garment exactly as you intend. A complete tech pack gets you an accurate costing, a right first time sample and fewer surprises in bulk.

Updated 2026-11-04 by the GoodBiz Apparels sourcing team

What a tech pack should include

SectionWhat to give
Flat sketchesFront and back drawings with seams, stitching and details called out
Measurement chartPoints of measure for your base size, with tolerances
GradingMeasurements for every size in your range
FabricComposition, construction, GSM and any finish, or a swatch
ColoursPantone TCX references or physical colour standards
Prints and artworkVector files with placement and size
TrimsButtons, zips, threads, elastic, with sizes and colours
LabelsBrand, size, care and fibre labels with positions
PackingFolding, polybag, size ratio per carton and carton marks
QuantitiesUnits per size and colour, and the destination port

If you do not have a tech pack

Send a reference garment and tell us what to change: fabric, colour, fit or details. We measure the garment, prepare a spec sheet and send it back for your written approval before cutting the sample.

What happens after you send it

  1. We review the tech pack and ask any questions in one message.
  2. We send a costing and a sample timeline.
  3. We make the sample and send photos and measurements, then the physical sample.
  4. You approve or comment. Bulk production starts only after written approval.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask

Anything else? Send us your question.

Can a manufacturer work without a tech pack?

Yes. A reference garment plus written changes is enough to start. The factory turns it into a spec sheet for your approval.

What file format should a tech pack be?

PDF is the most common. Include vector artwork files (AI, SVG or PDF) separately for prints and labels.

Ready to source?

Send your spec or ask what is in stock. We reply with availability or a production costing.
